Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled **"Multi-environment genomic
prediction under genotype × environment interaction: a simulation study
validated in CIMMYT wheat"** for consideration as an Original Article.

Genotype × environment interaction (G×E) is a central obstacle to genomic
prediction in plant breeding, yet the conditions under which multi-environment
models actually outperform simpler alternatives are not well delineated. In this
work we (i) benchmark four prediction models — single-environment GBLUP, the
multi-environment phenotypic mean, a reduced-rank reaction-norm model, and
multi-trait GBLUP — across a factorial simulation in which the type-B genetic
correlation (rG = 0.2–0.8) and heritability (h² = 0.2–0.6) were explicitly
controlled; and (ii) validate the conclusions on the public CIMMYT wheat panel
of Crossa et al. (2010). Multi-trait GBLUP was consistently the most accurate
method; its advantage grows with rG and is largest at low heritability, whereas
the reaction-norm model adds nothing under unstructured G×E. The real-data
results reproduce the simulation ranking exactly.

We believe the paper is a good fit for *TAG* because it addresses a practical
modelling decision faced by every breeding programme, provides a controlled,
reproducible benchmark, and validates it against a widely used public data set.
The full pipeline is open and reproducible; a model- and data-verification
report accompanies the manuscript.

We confirm that this manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not
under consideration by another journal. All authors approved the submission and
declare no competing interests.

Thank you for your consideration.
